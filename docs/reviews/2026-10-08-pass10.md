Lessons for the next landing:

- **Build the alarm before the wait, not after the gap.** The loop went silent for three hours (01:13Z to 04:03Z)
  because the landing script had stopped on a CI failure and a long classification had been started with a bare
  `nohup`: neither could wake the coordinator. A Monitor on the landing log's `STOPPED`, `MERGED`, `FAILED` and
  stall lines, re-armed every 30 minutes, and every long job started as a background task that notifies on exit,
  is now the rule; the user asked for it in those words. One alarm was still wired wrong: the loop's "exited"
  notice keyed on `landq.sh` ending, and `landq.sh` by design never ends (with nothing left to land it sleeps and
  re-reads its list every minute, so a PR can be appended while it runs), so the last merge was reported by the
  Monitor's `ALL LANDED` line alone and never pushed to the user's device. The completion alarm belongs on that
  line, and a milestone like the last merge gets a push notification, not only a line in the chat.
- **A batch stacks its PRs, so a hunk conflict inside the batch is a hand merge onto the previous PR's head, not
  onto `main`.** Three of the five hand stops were between batch members (#1101 onto #1100, #1103 onto #1101,
  #1132 onto #1106); a conflict there must be resolved on the stacked base and the rebase finished to its last
  commit before any push.
- **Check `git status` before a force-push after a conflict.** #1132's resolution was pushed after the first
  `rebase --continue` with two of its four commits still to apply; the branch was truncated for four minutes until
  the remaining CHANGELOG conflicts were resolved and the full rebase pushed. `git rebase --show-current-patch`
  or a clean `git status` is the gate.
- **A fix can make a sibling's mutation moot.** #1103's canonical rendering removed the defect that #1030's
  `archive_whole_partial_compared_as_text` planted, so its guard passed against the mutant on the rebased head and
  the discriminate track failed; the mutation was re-pointed at a grid where the value and the text still differ.
- **A non-strict SELECT INTO over a non-unique predicate is a flake with a plan-shaped trigger.** tests/295's
  helpers (new in #1106) picked the regrain's child by `lo = '0'` alone; once the first fine copy is recorded,
  unattached, under the same lo, the row the lookup returns is the first of whatever plan it gets, and the
  shared test database's statistics choose between a bitmap scan (the monolith first) and an index scan (the
  copy first). It failed two merge groups on the dbdev channel five hours apart, passed on rerun once and
  stopped the landing the second time; fixed by `and attached` (#1136, #1137) and landed ahead of the remaining
  PRs, proven by forcing the index plan (five assertions red on the old file, none on the new).
- **A guard that plants its own defect by text is a mutation too, and a sibling's signature change moves it.**
  #1128's read-back guard patches the Parquet strategy's PUT by a three-line pattern that includes the overwrite
  refusal's call; #1112 gave that call a digest argument, so on the rebased tree the pattern matched zero times
  and the guard refused to run ("pgpm_archive/install.sql moved; fix the pattern"), the archive track failed and
  the loop stopped. The pattern's own exactly-once discipline is what caught it; the re-anchor kept the refusal
  line because the two PUT lines alone match `archive.to_s3_parquet`'s as well.
- **Cancelled PR runs need no rerun once `main` has moved.** Under runner starvation (47 queued runs org-wide at
  20:07Z, a merge group timing out at 60 minutes with its summary job never scheduled) the queued PR-level runs of
  the PRs not yet at their turn were cancelled to give the group runners; because `land.sh` rebases each PR onto
  the moved `main` at its turn, every one got fresh runs then.
- **A guard database number is a shared resource.** Twenty-four of the twenty-five PRs were briefed with
  `pgpm_perf100` to `pgpm_perf128`, which collide with existing guards because the coordinator's "max finder"
  sort was wrong (the real maximum was 297); `land.sh` renumbers duplicates at the rebase, so nothing broke, but
  the next brief reads the maximum with `sort -V`.
